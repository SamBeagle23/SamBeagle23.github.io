On Site Tracking

Building a GPS tracker designed for athletes. This tracker is to be easy to use, miniature, and able to be laced into a cleat.

Process

User research

When interviewing college athletes, I received a lot of different feedback about equipment and resources available. These differences were mainly due to a difference in funding and scholarships, but they impacted them in a big way.

As a case study, I documented the user journey of different college football athletes. This helped me identify their main pain points, but it also led me to find discrepancies in how they function. Below is a documented journey map of their average day, but there was still much for me to learn about these athletes following my initial interview process.

Physical development

Sketching flexible yet smooth forms was important. A visible power button and distinctive shape was initially very crucial. Later, when developing the final form, the ability to feed laces through the device was the most important detail.

Prototyping

This device was built in Fusion 360, printed with Bambu Lab and rendered with Blender and KeyShot. I refined and added details to these renders with Adobe Firefly.

Sizing

Experimenting with different widths through printing and trying on real shoes.

Ideation + feedback

Wearable tracking is everything in sports metric tracking, and this was an area I saw very large discrepancies in. Youth athletes and lower-level college athletes don’t have any GPS tracking capabilities, yet private high schools and large college programs spend millions on it a year. The issue with these devices is the competition to be the most cutting-edge tracker. Real athletes don’t need every new piece of tech or trackable metric. They need simple GPS data in an affordable package. The question is, how can an athlete or program improve the way they operate altogether without breaking the bank? Below I prototyped some fluid solutions.

These athletes, from D1 FBS, D1 FCS, and JUCO schools respectively, gave important feedback. This led me to developing a wearable tracker that was easy to use and easy to adopt into a program. I wanted to build a tracker that was…

  • Captivating
  • Attachable
  • Simple
  • Scalable
  • Multi-purpose
  • Affordable
  • Sensical
